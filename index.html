How to use your workspace

A visual walkthrough of the actual analysis flow and its controls.

01 · Add the agreed scope

Paste the proposal, SOW, contract or clearly agreed deliverables. Include revision limits, exclusions, approval rules and dependencies where available.

01 · Agreed scope / project terms
Website includes Home, About and Services. One revision round. New functionality requires written approval.

Why it matters: this is the evidence BoundaryFlow compares each client request against. Do not add assumptions as if they were contract terms.

02 · Paste the latest client message

Paste the client's message as received, including requests, explanations and statements about what they believe is included.

02 · New client message
Please add a booking form. I assumed it was included because the site is responsive.

BoundaryFlow separates actionable work from client claims instead of treating every sentence as a request.

03 · Choose a response style and analyze

Select Professional, Warm & collaborative, or Clear & firm. This changes communication tone, not the underlying scope decision. Then select Analyze Request.

04 · Read the decision, evidence and risk

Each request is evaluated separately. Read the status, confidence, boundary risk, agreement evidence, explanation and next move. A client claim is displayed separately. It is an assertion, not automatically misinformation: compare it with relevant agreement evidence and classify it as supported, contradicted or unverified.

Confidence describes evidential support. Boundary risk describes the freelancer's exposure. They are not interchangeable.

05 · Use targeted clarification

When a request is unclear, BoundaryFlow should identify the exact missing term, formulate a client-ready question and explain how different answers could affect the decision.

Targeted scope clarification
Does the agreed revision include adding this new section, or is it limited to existing elements?
Possible outcomes: included · additional · remain unclear

06 · Additional information and reanalysis

Add the actual clarification, message or relevant record in the text field, then re-analyze. You do not need to choose an evidence-source category. BoundaryFlow examines the wording, who is described as agreeing, whether the information relates to the specific request, and whether it satisfies the agreement’s approval conditions.

Additional information
Paste the actual client clarification, written record or relevant context…
Use this information & re-analyze

Follow-up questions are selected from the actual client claim and the missing evidence. An assumption, a claimed meeting promise, a claimed email approval and supplied ready-to-publish content should not all produce the same question. When a client says an approval email exists, provide the actual email thread for review; a summary of the email is not the email itself.

A statement is not proof merely because it is labelled as approved. Equally, an unsupported claim is not automatically misinformation. BoundaryFlow preserves the original agreement, assesses the actual content and explains what is supported, contradicted or still unverified. It does not independently authenticate identity, signatures or legal validity.

07 · Resolve missing approval and evidence conflicts

When the original agreement contains an exclusion or approval condition, later information must be checked against that exact condition. A later statement must be assessed by its actual wording and context. It must identify the relevant change and establish the approval required by the original agreement; merely saying that something was agreed is not enough.

Example · testimonial section excluded unless separately approved in writing
Later note: the freelancer says the section is included, but no written approval changing the exclusion is supplied.
Scope conflict
Missing: explicit written approval from both parties identifying the testimonial section and changing or overriding the original exclusion.
Ask: “I can put together a short change proposal for the Home-page testimonial section you requested, with up to three testimonials. Would you like me to send that over for your review and written approval before I begin?”

BoundaryFlow should preserve both records, explain the missing approval, and recommend not implementing the work until the conflict is resolved. If no approval exists, agree the change before proceeding. If the records remain disputed or cannot be verified, keep the decision unresolved. The freelancer remains responsible for checking the underlying documents.

08 · Ask naturally, about the actual change

Client questions should sound like a real project conversation and name the exact item being discussed. Do not ask generic questions such as “Which scope version governs?” when the missing detail is a page, colour, section, button, form or other specific deliverable.

Examples of targeted questions
Page: “Are you asking for a completely new Pricing page, or an update to the existing Services page?”
Colour: “Do you have a specific colour or hex code in mind, or should I choose a shade that works with the existing site palette?”
Testimonials: “I can prepare a short change proposal for the Home-page testimonial section you requested, with up to three testimonials. Would you like me to send that over for review and written approval before I begin?”

First identify whether the missing item is a design choice, technical detail, content decision, scope approval or commercial decision. Ask only for that missing item. If the work is clearly outside scope, offer a practical next step such as preparing a change proposal; do not ask the client to restate a request they have already made. Never invent prices, timelines or details that the client has not supplied.
